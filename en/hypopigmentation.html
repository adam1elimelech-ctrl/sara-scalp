Hypopigmentation – Medical Tattoo for Filling Pigment-Deficient Areas

Hypopigmentation Camouflage Tattooing – restoring a natural tone to white or light areas of the skin

Book a Hypopigmentation Consultation

Hypopigmentation is a condition in which areas of the skin lose part or all of their pigment and appear white or significantly lighter than the surrounding skin. This can occur after psoriasis, burns, inflammation, injury, or other conditions. Treatment with medical tattooing (Hypopigmentation Camouflage Tattooing) allows these areas to be filled with a precise tone that blends with the skin, restoring a more even and natural appearance. At Sara Scalp in Jerusalem we specialize in this treatment with a team trained in Brazil and Turkey.

What Causes Hypopigmentation?

There are many causes: skin conditions such as psoriasis or vitiligo (in certain cases), burns that damaged melanocytes, scars, severe inflammation, certain medical treatments, or even prolonged sun exposure on damaged areas. The result is spots or areas that draw attention because of the color contrast. For many people this affects self-confidence, especially in summer or when wearing revealing clothes.

Medical tattooing does not cure the underlying medical condition, but it offers a stable aesthetic solution that reduces the visibility of the light areas. The work requires high precision in tone matching, because every skin is different and every area responds differently.

How Is the Treatment Performed?

In the initial consultation we examine the areas, their size, the current tone and the surrounding skin. Then careful color matching is performed. The pigment is implanted in delicate layers, often over several sessions, in order to build the color gradually and ensure it remains natural and stable. We check the result in natural light and from different angles to avoid a “painted” look or sharp borders.

The treatment is suitable for stable areas. If there is an active medical condition (such as psoriasis in a flare), we may recommend waiting or consulting a dermatologist before starting treatment.

Who Is It Suitable For?

  • Anyone with white areas after psoriasis or skin inflammation
  • Hypopigmentation after burns or injury
  • Light areas after medical treatments
  • Anyone who wants to reduce the contrast between pigment-deficient areas and healthy skin

The most successful results are achieved when the tone is matched precisely, the work is done patiently, and the client follows the home-care instructions. Many clients report that they feel much more comfortable with themselves, especially during the summer months.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the treatment suitable for vitiligo? +
In certain cases of stable vitiligo, medical tattooing can be considered, but individual assessment and sometimes consultation with a dermatologist are required. Not every case is suitable.
How long do the results last? +
The pigment is stable for years. Gradual fading may occur, and a refresh can be performed as needed.
